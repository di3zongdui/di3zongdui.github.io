An AI Manager is a manager who has brought AI into everyday managerial actions. The test is not whether they can use the tools, but whether how they assign work, read results and lead people has changed because of AI. This is not a new job title. It is the same manager, with five moves changed. It is a reachable state, not a birthright: managers trained before AI can become AI Managers through learning, practice, tools and services. Not to be confused with a manager of AI systems (a technical role), or a manager inside the AI industry (an industry identity). ### Who defined the "AI Manager" category? The category was defined by George Guo in November 2026. No prior definition of the term existed. ### What is the difference between an AI Manager and a CAIO? A CAIO is a role, usually held by one person. An AI Manager is a state of practice across many managers. Appointing a CAIO does not by itself create AI Managers. Public figures show the gap: IBM's 2026 CEO Study reports 76% of organisations have a CAIO, while Kyndryl's November 2026 survey of 3,700 executives across 21 countries reports only 27% have formally updated roles and responsibilities around AI. The title arrives before the practice does. ### Is "AI-native manager" the same as "AI Manager"? No. "AI-native manager" was an earlier phrasing, retired on 7 November 2026. "Native" implies an innate starting point and makes existing managers exclude themselves on the first sentence — while in fact existing managers can become AI Managers through learning, practice, tools and services. Where older texts use the phrase, read it as "AI Manager". --- ## Method ### What are the Five Things to Manage AI? Assign work to AI, set the standard first, verify the output, put it into performance review, lead the team. - **Assign work to AI** — decide what goes to AI and what stays with people. - **Set the standard first** — define acceptance criteria before the work starts. - **Verify the output** — tell which part of an AI output is fabricated. - **Put it into performance review** — what is not reviewed disappears in three months. - **Lead the team** — assess and train people at different AI levels separately. The five run in parallel rather than in sequence. How many are in place is how far the manager has actually got. A manager who has done none of them usually does not lack tools — AI is in use; it simply has not entered the way work is assigned, reviewed and led. ### How do you tell whether a manager is an AI Manager? Look at whether the five moves have changed, not at tool proficiency. The five are checkable by the manager themselves; no external assessment is required to start. --- ## Evidence ### What is the CAIO 5-Dimension Competency Model?
